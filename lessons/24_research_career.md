# Lesson 24 — Defend your work and build a research practice

Prerequisites: Lessons 22–23. Suggested effort: 5–8 hours, including the lab. Project: 8.

## The professional deliverable is a decision with evidence

A strong research memo leads with the question and result, gives enough method to assess the evidence, states what could explain it and recommends a specific action. It is neither a literature dump nor a promotional backtest. A negative result can demonstrate excellent judgment if its data and evaluation are rigorous and its conclusion is appropriately scoped.

For an oral defense, prepare answers at three levels: a 30-second decision, a three-minute evidence summary and a deeper technical discussion. Be able to derive your scoring rule, trace one feature's availability, explain a failed hypothesis and name the assumption most likely to change the recommendation. Do not hide behind architecture complexity when asked about units or leakage.

Your portfolio should show distinct competencies: an independent real-data audit, temporal/execution correctness, a baseline comparison, an AI evidence pipeline, risk/optimization, restricted IRL, reproducible engineering and an independently reviewed capstone. Keep private or licensed data out of public artifacts; publish permissible fixtures, schemas and instructions instead. A reproducible negative result is stronger than a screenshot of a high Sharpe with inaccessible inputs.

## Worked presentation structure

Slide 1: question, decision and strongest limitation. Slide 2: population and information clock. Slide 3: mechanism and baseline. Slide 4: frozen experimental procedure. Slide 5: primary result with uncertainty and sample counts. Slide 6: costs, exposures and missing execution evidence. Slide 7: challenges, failures and sensitivity. Slide 8: next experiment and stop rule. For the sample capstone, the title should say public-response forecast audit, not successful investor-intent discovery.

A first 90-day research plan should start with the desk's actual decision and infrastructure: reproduce a known result, own a bounded data/measurement problem, propose one controlled extension, and earn trust through clear reviews. Trading desks differ by horizon, asset, mandate and data entitlement. This course builds a core equity-research foundation; specialist derivatives pricing, exchange connectivity or large-scale systems require additional focused work.

## Lab procedure

1. Write an 800-word capstone memo and an eight-slide text storyboard. No presentation software is required.
2. Record or rehearse a ten-minute defense, then answer the question bank without reading the key.
3. Build a portfolio index linking contracts, code, tests, results and limitations for all eight projects.
4. Write a first 90-day plan for one specific role: predictive research, execution research or research engineering. State which skill gaps remain.
5. Revisit one primary reading that materially changed your design and explain the change with a concrete example.

## Assignments

**A1.** Write your 30-second decision and three-minute evidence outline. Include a result, uncertainty, scope and next action.

**A2.** Submit the final portfolio index, capstone memo, storyboard and 90-day plan. Complete the graduation rubric and list unresolved skill gaps with a practice plan.

**A3.** Answer five defense questions: Why this target? What was knowable then? What is the strongest baseline? What would invalidate the inference? What would justify more data or compute? Use your actual artifacts in each answer.

Mastery check: you can explain the work clearly to both a researcher and a decision-maker, and your evidence supports every claimed competency.
