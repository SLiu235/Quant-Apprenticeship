# Lesson 24 answer key — Defend your work and build a research practice

Attempt the assignments before reading. Different code is welcome; the evidence and reasoning must agree.

## A1

Worked 30-second answer: “On 139 selected issuer-days across 52 dates, the frozen text feature worsened public-minus-market NLL by about .01465 per equally weighted date. The exploratory three-date block interval is positive. Missing receipt/edit history and engagement-conditioned comment collection further limit interpretation. I would not promote the model; a prospectively logged panel is the next candidate experiment if its acquisition cost is justified.”

Three-minute outline: contract and data → timing/population limits → matched baselines and temporal selection → paired primary result → absent execution/actor evidence → decision and next-information cost. Avoid claiming the interval proves all social data useless.

## A2

Use the assessment rubric: complete evidence matters more than a polished deck. The portfolio must include source/experiment lineage and failed hypotheses. A practical 90-day plan: days 1–30 reproduce one desk result and map data/decision controls; 31–60 own a bounded baseline or measurement repair with review; 61–90 deliver one independently replicated extension or justified negative decision. Choose measurable artifacts and stakeholder reviews, not “learn all finance.” Remaining gaps may include exchange-specific production systems, richer asset classes, market-impact estimation or domain-specific data access.

## A3

Strong answers cite your own contract, availability ledger, baseline comparison, assumption audit and resource budget. For the sample pilot: target is market response because actor actions are absent; availability is partly assumed rather than observed; frequency and market are essential controls; collection/timing defects restrict inference; more compute is not justified before a data or model question specifies what it resolves. A professional answer can be “we cannot establish that from this evidence,” followed by a concrete acquisition or experiment plan.

Repair: rehearse with every unsupported adjective removed. Replace “robust,” “realistic” or “state of the art” with the exact checks that warrant the claim.
